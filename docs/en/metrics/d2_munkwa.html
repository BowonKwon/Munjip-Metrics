Examination linkage and age at passing

Share of authors linked to a passer in the civil-examination roster, and the distribution of the age at passing (East Asian reckoning) for linked authors.

49.1%of authors linked to the examination roster
Key point

49.1% of the 1,248 authors (613) link to a passer in the civil-examination roster (name and birth year 546, first pass of a re-examined person 65, courtesy or pen name 2): 83% of those born in the 15th century, 62% (16th), 52% (17th), 30% (18th), 22% (19th). An unlinked author is not necessarily a non-passer; variant-character and namesake candidates are reviewed separately. Median age at passing is 30 (27 in the 15th century, 35 in the 18th).

Formula
Names NFKC-normalized (煕=熙 included). M1 same name and birth year (several rows of one clan seat: first pass = M1-first) / M2 same name, roster birth absent, courtesy or pen name identical / M3 same name, single candidate, roster birth absent, exam year within [birth+15, death (or birth+70)]. More than one survivor: unresolved. Linkage = (M1+M1-first+M2+M3) / authors.
Caveats
Linkage is not a pass rate: authors who never passed the examination (rural scholars, monks, women, Goryeo authors) rightly stay unlinked. The short introductions rarely mention examinations, so precision could not be measured against them; a courtesy- and pen-name cross-check stands in (reliability card).
Reliability card
sampledSampled precision — accuracy of title parsing or person linkage measured on a coded sample

The first plan for linkage precision (checking the examination statement in the short introductions) gave no signal: only 1 of 300 sampled introductions mentions a pass. Instead, among the 179 linked authors whose courtesy or pen name is given on both sides (a comparable subset of the 613; for the 2 M2 links the same names were used for linking), 93.3% (167) agree on at least one; the 12 disagreements include variant characters and alternative pen names. This is not an independent precision for all links.
