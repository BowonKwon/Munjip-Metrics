Share of court documents

Share of own works in the court-document class (公車類: memorials, examination answers, royal edicts), by century.

5.6%of own works are court documents
Key point

Court documents (memorials, examination answers, edicts) are 5.6% of own works: 42% in the 9th century (Choe Chi-won), 9.1% and 8.7% in the 12th and 13th, 5.8% and 7.7% in the 16th and 17th, 2.4% in the 19th.

Formula
Court-document works / own works
Caveats
Documents submitted to or issued by the court, plus examination answers (對策), so the genre is tied to office and examinations; the authors' careers were not verified on this site.
Reliability card
exactExact count — read straight from the source XML

Classification quality: a sample of 300 own works was judged from titles for whether the ITKC major class fits; 97.3% fit (Wilson 95% 94.8%–98.6%; 3 undecided counted as disagreement; a second coder re-judged 30 of them blind (without the first judgments) with 97% agreement). The sampler meant to take up to six works per class × century cell, but it filled the cells in order and stopped at 300, so the later classes were barely reached (poetry 8, miscellany 0). The rate is the result for the 300 works examined and cannot be used to estimate classification quality across poetry or miscellany; the make-up of the sample and its gaps also limit any generalization to all own works. Known block error: 284 works of 湖陰雜稿 (ITKC_MO_0126A) filed as 冊文類 carry poem titles; they are left as classified and noted here.

Words used on this pageCoding, coder — reading the texts one by one and assigning each a type, or a yes or no, by rules written down beforehand (the codebook). The coder is whoever makes those judgments; on this site, a language model (AI).
Blind — whoever judges (a coder or a verifier) did so without seeing what the judgment would be compared with (the first coder's judgment, the author's reasoning and so on), so agreement cannot come from copying. A blind re-coding is a second coder judging the same texts that way.
95% interval (confidence interval) — the range in which the true rate for the whole plausibly lies, given the rate measured on a sample. With random samples, ranges built the same way again and again contain the true value about 95 times in 100. Other things equal, the smaller the sample, the wider the range.
Wilson — the name of the method used to compute that range; it still works when the sample is small or the rate is near 0% or 100%.

Reading the by-century charts: left to right is the century of the author's birth, and each point is computed over all the collections of authors born in that century. No collection has an author born in the 10th century or 11th century, so the line breaks there. The points for the 9th century (2 collections), 12th century (2 collections), 13th century (7 collections) rest on only a few collections.
