Sources#

Institute for the Translation of Korean Classics, 'Hanguk munjip chonggan' (data.go.kr dataset 3074298, no license restriction): 1,249 XML archives adopted from the 1,251 portal entries (one without an attachment and one old-schema edition excluded), one per collection — a bibliographic file (author, series volume, publication year, table of contents, short introduction) plus one file per fascicle with every work's title, writer, genre class and text. Counted at the work level (type="최종정보").

Academy of Korean Studies, 'Successful candidates of the civil service examination' (15052752): 15,151 passers with name, courtesy and pen names, clan seat, birth year, examination year and grade. Academy of Korean Studies, 'Korean historical persons': 27,035 persons with names, courtesy and pen names, dates and clan seat; used to cross-check author dates.

Unit of counting#

Every number is counted per work: one level element of type="최종정보" in the ITKC XML. Of 637,203 works, 609,494 are by the collection's author (writer name equals the bibliographic author). Characters are CJK code points inside <내용>; those inside original, collation and marginal notes are counted separately as note characters. Check: for all 1,249 collections the count equals an independent recount of every <내용> in the raw XML; zero residual character references.

Genres#

The genre classification the ITKC attached to each work (7 major, 28 minor classes) is used as given; nothing was corrected or added. Classification quality is shown by the residual 'other' share (B7) and by the share of a 300-work sample judged, from the title (and the opening of the text where needed), to fit its class, reported on the reliability card (the rate is the result for the 300 works examined and cannot be used to estimate classification quality across poetry or miscellany; the sample's limits are on the card) (marked as in progress where not yet done in 1.0).

Centuries#

The XML carries no date per work, so every time series is grouped by the author's birth century. Printing lags the author's active years by about a century (Kwak 2026; publication lag D3). Collections whose author has no birth year sit in a 'birth year unknown' row.

Merging authors#

The author id is the Chinese-character name plus the birth year. Several collections by one author (main collection, continuation, posthumous works) are summed in the author table with the number of collections shown. Works whose writer differs from the author (prefaces, postscripts, biographies, elegies, appendices) are excluded from the author's output. Two different people sharing a name and birth year cannot be told apart; such cases are corrected through overrides when found.

Person linkage#

Examination-roster rules: M1 same name and birth year (first pass when a person is listed for re-examinations), M2 same name, roster birth absent, courtesy or pen name identical, M3 same name, a single candidate, examination year within the lifetime. Where more than one candidate survives the author stays unlinked (AMB). Linkage is not a pass rate. The AKS person database is matched by name and birth year (P1) or name and pen name (P2) and used only to check death years.

Relationship genres#

A letter's addressee is the string after an opening 答·與·上·寄·復·報·奉·呈·贈·簡·謝 up to 書·簡·問目·別紙·狀 etc.; an epitaph's subject is the string before 墓誌銘·墓碣銘·神道碑·行狀 etc. with honorific prefixes removed. Version 1.0 does not merge strings into persons: the same person named by courtesy name, pen name or office counts as different addressees. Parsing precision is measured on a sample.

Known limits#

  • Defects of the XML (page-number mismatches, additions, omissions, characters differing from the page images; Choi et al. 2021) were not corrected; page numbers are not used in any metric.
  • The size of a 'work' is an editorial decision; where poem cycles are bundled, work-based shares diverge from character-based ones.
  • Named-entity tags vary in density from 0 to 100% between collections and are not used in any metric.
  • The 8 authors without a birth year (841 works, 709 own) appear as a separate 'birth year unknown' row in the series A and B century tables and are absent from the series C and D century tables (whose sums cover dated authors only); the headline totals (1,248 authors, 637,203 works, 609,494 own works) include them.
  • 284 works of the Hoeum japgo (ITKC_MO_0126A) filed under 冊文類 carry poem titles — a block error in the ITKC classification. They are counted as classified and noted on the series B reliability cards. 88 works without a genre class (five late supplement collections) enter denominators only.
  • Translation and collation links are tags of the 2024-08 XML and may differ from the current state of the programs.
  • No interpretation is offered; no author or school is evaluated.

Reproduction#

The GitHub repository's src/ holds the fetcher (fetch_itkc.py), work table (build_works.py), checks (check_works.py), author linkage (link_authors.py), metrics (metrics_a–d.py), reliability (reliability.py) and site (build_site.py). The portal lists 1,251 entries; 1,250 attachments were downloaded (one entry has none) and 1,249 archives adopted (one old-schema duplicate excluded); their SHA-256 digests are in raw_manifest.json.

Romanization#

Collection titles and personal names follow the Revised Romanization of Korean (2000); the surnames 김 and 이 are given in their customary forms, Kim and Yi.

Terms used on this page#

Coding, coder — reading the texts one by one and assigning each a type, or a yes or no, by rules written down beforehand (the codebook). The coder is whoever makes those judgments; on this site, a language model (AI).
Blind — whoever judges (a coder or a verifier) did so without seeing what the judgment would be compared with (the first coder's judgment, the author's reasoning and so on), so agreement cannot come from copying. A blind re-coding is a second coder judging the same texts that way.
95% interval (confidence interval) — the range in which the true rate for the whole plausibly lies, given the rate measured on a sample. With random samples, ranges built the same way again and again contain the true value about 95 times in 100. Other things equal, the smaller the sample, the wider the range.
Wilson — the name of the method used to compute that range; it still works when the sample is small or the rate is near 0% or 100%.
Precision — of the items a rule (a keyword or a string rule) picked out, the share that are right. Higher means fewer false hits. On this site it is mostly measured by coders judging a sample of the items picked out.
Recall — of the items that should have been found, the share that were found. Higher means fewer misses.
Parsing, parser — cutting a string such as a title by fixed rules to pull out the part needed (an addressee's name, for instance). The parser is the program that does it.
HHI (Herfindahl index) — a measure of concentration: the sum of the squared shares. It is 1 when everything is in one place and falls toward 0 as the total is spread evenly over more places.
Stratified sample — the whole is split into groups (periods, classes and so on) and a set number is drawn at random from each, so that the sample does not bunch up in one group. When the same number is drawn from groups of different size, the rate over the whole sample has to be weighted by group size before it stands for the whole.
